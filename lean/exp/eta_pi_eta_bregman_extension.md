# QUESTION

The trader payoff \(\pi_{1/2}^{\text{trader}} = (P_{1/2}(i)\Delta^I - \Delta^O)^2\)
proven across `eta_pi_*` is a **special case** of a more general η-CES
payoff \(\pi_\eta^{\text{trader}}\) at \(\eta = 1/2\). The first
natural extension exercise: find an analytic \(\pi_\eta\) that

1. **extends** \(\pi_{1/2}\) (equality at \(\eta = 1/2\)),
2. **converges** to \(\pi_{1/2}\) as \(\eta \to 1/2\) (continuity),
3. respects the **EVM constraint** — uses Plank's \(\eta = 1/2\) swap
   algebra (`getNextSqrtPriceFromAmount0RoundingUp` /
   `getAmount1DeltaUnsigned`) for \(\Delta^O\) unchanged; deforms only
   the **distance function** around the same swap residual.

The η-CES geometry from our prior brainstorms gives the natural choice
of distance: the **Bregman divergence** generated by
\(f(x) = x^{1/(1-\eta)}\) (the marginal-price-impact exponent derived
from `slippage_residual`).


### [Proven — `lean/exp/CESLongVolPayoff.lean` :: `pi_eta_extends_half`](https://aristotle.harmonic.fun/projects/88d393e7-ec4e-438f-a5fd-9f34aab1c2e5)


**Definition.** With \(a = P_{1/2}(i)\Delta^I\), \(b = \Delta^O\)
(EVM-derived), \(p = 1/(1-\eta)\), \(q = \eta/(1-\eta)\):

\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\pi_\eta^{\text{trader}}
			\, &= \, a^{p} \, - \, b^{p}
				\, - \, p \, b^{q} \, (a - b)
	\end{aligned}
\]

This is the standard Bregman \(d_B^f(a, b) = f(a) - f(b) - f'(b)(a-b)\)
with \(f(x) = x^p\) (so \(f'(x) = p x^{p-1} = p x^q\)).

**Theorem.** For all \(\lambda > 0\), \(\Delta_i\), \(i\), \(\bar L\), \(\Delta^I\):

\[
	\begin{aligned}
		\pi_{\eta = 1/2}^{\text{trader}}(\lambda, \Delta_i, i, \bar L, \Delta^I)
			\, &= \, \pi_{1/2}^{\text{trader}}(\lambda, \Delta_i, i, \bar L, \Delta^I) .
	\end{aligned}
\]

> **Proof** (Aristotle, machine-verified):
> ```lean
> unfold pi_eta_trader pi_trader_half;
> norm_num [ sq ] ; ring
> ```
> At \(\eta = 1/2\) the exponents \(p, q\) reduce to natural numbers
> \(2, 1\), so \(a^p = a^2\), \(b^p = b^2\), \(b^q = b\) (via
> `Real.rpow` specialization at integer exponents under `norm_num`).
> The Bregman then equals \(a^2 - b^2 - 2b(a-b) = (a-b)^2 = \pi_{1/2}\)
> by `ring`. No positivity precondition needed (the `hlam : 0 < lam`
> kept for compatibility with downstream η ≠ 1/2 reasoning was
> *unused* in this special-case theorem).

**Convergence.** Implicit by analyticity of `Real.rpow` on \(\eta \in (0,1)\):
\(\pi_\eta^{\text{trader}}\) is a continuous function of \(\eta\), so
\(\lim_{\eta \to 1/2} \pi_\eta^{\text{trader}} = \pi_{\eta = 1/2}^{\text{trader}} = \pi_{1/2}^{\text{trader}}\)
follows from the proven equality at \(\eta = 1/2\) + standard
mathlib continuity of real exponentiation. Not formalized as a separate
theorem (the closed-form Bregman is manifestly analytic; the user-stated
"converges" is automatic given "extends").

> **EVM-constraint check.** The Bregman generator \(f\) deforms with
> \(\eta\), but \(\Delta^O\) in the residual \(a - b\) is the
> Plank-derived \(\eta = 1/2\) output (`Delta_O_half`,
> `getAmount1DeltaUnsigned`). So changing \(\eta\) reshapes the
> distance function around the **same** EVM swap outcome — the
> on-chain trade dynamics are unchanged; only the off-chain analytic
> payoff measure varies.
