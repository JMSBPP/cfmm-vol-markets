## Design decisions the plan must put to the user (not decide unilaterally)

1. **Statement-class taxonomy — SETTLED 2026-08-03 (user pick: "Rule").** Four classes, per-class
   counters: **Definition** (mints an object), **Rule** (normative — a protocol stipulation;
   alternatives were possible; the `←` arrow is Rule syntax; the implementation complies or not),
   **Proposition/Theorem** (asserts a fact; Aristotle proves or refutes; separate counters per the
   user's earlier ruling), **Convention** (how we WRITE; no runtime content). The classifying test:
   who could disagree, and what would settle it. Retroactive homes when reached: the streamia
   identification, the τ_JIT tax law, and the decided maturity law are Rules currently dressed as
   definitions.
2. **Numbering scheme.** Global (`Definition 1, 2, …`) vs section-scoped (`Definition E.1`)?
   Interacts with the M/E/J/G labels below and with Phase 14's ordering constraint (12.1 strictly
   before or strictly after the V-blocks — renumbering twice is the failure mode).
3. **Fate of the M/E/J/G block labels.** They are cited everywhere (traceability rows, commit
   messages, Aristotle prompts, this repo's planning). Options: (a) keep them as the section spine
   and put numbered statements INSIDE blocks — no citation breaks; (b) replace with pure
   Definition/Proposition numbering — clean but breaks every existing cross-reference. (a) is the
   conservative recommendation.
4. **The symbol structure.** One consolidated notation section at the head (promoting the parameter
   block + collecting the notation-map lines), or per-section notation blocks kept as-is with a
   master index? The binding rules (ε/σ reservation, ℙ_event, define-before-writing) become the
   PREAMBLE of that section either way.
5. **Lean citation style.** Keep `> LEAN (proved, …)` quote-blocks attached to statements, or move to
   a statement-footer convention (`*Formalized:* \`EtaCurvature.etaStar\` …`)? The traceability doc
   is the machine-facing twin either way (per the standing human-entry-point rule).
